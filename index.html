The world warms by one number. Your city warms by another.

Under the high emissions pathway, Cape Town ends the century about 3 degrees warmer. Tehran ends it about 7. Both are in the same global average.

Every model agrees on the direction

There is no real argument about whether these cities get warmer. Averaged across all thirty, both pathways rise, and the gap between them is the choice we make about emissions.

But the average is not where anyone lives

Drag the year and the map recolors. Switch the pathway and watch which places pull away from the pack first. Click any city to pin it.

And the models do not agree on how much

The direction is settled. The size is not. For the same city and the same pathway, the four models land in a wide band, and that band is the honest uncertainty.

Takeaway

A global average is a budget, not a forecast for a place. The same 5 degrees of global warming is 3 degrees in one city and 7 in another, and the spread between models is almost as wide as the spread between pathways. If you want to know what warming means for somewhere, you have to ask about somewhere.
